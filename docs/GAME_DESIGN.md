# VEYAL — Game Design

**Status:** Initial design baseline. Some features described here are planned, not yet implemented.

## 1. High concept

VEYAL is a real-time strategy and survival game. The player builds an energy network, establishes defences, and destroys Ooze sources before the advancing mass overwhelms the battlefield.

Terrain channels movement, water occupies low ground, and the Ooze accumulates in depressions. The player must build a defensive network while the threat continues to spread.

VEYAL is an independent work with original implementation, assets, terminology, balance, and presentation.

## 2. Design pillars

1. A living battlefield: terrain and fluid movement shape tactical decisions.
2. Readable cause and effect: players should understand why water and Ooze moved.
3. Pressure and prioritisation: sources are objectives; the player cannot defend everywhere.
4. Replayability: procedural maps and seeds create different tactical situations.
5. Accessible technology: browser play and offline operation.
6. Expandability: community maps and scenarios can be added later.

## 3. Core gameplay loop

1. Inspect terrain, water, energy opportunities, and visible Ooze sources.
2. Establish energy production and a connected network.
3. Build or activate defences.
4. Contain or destroy sources while managing expansion and energy.
5. Adapt to terrain channels, water displacement, and new routes.
6. Destroy all required sources before the Ooze reaches the defeat condition.

Structure types, costs, ranges, and balance should be tuned through prototypes rather than fixed prematurely.

## 4. The Veyal Ooze

- The Ooze is a spreading fluid-like threat generated by sources.
- Sources are objectives that can be destroyed.
- A source continues generating Ooze even when completely submerged.
- Submerging a source does not disable it.
- Ooze accumulates in depressions and valleys before rising onto surrounding land.
- When Ooze rises above the local water surface, it can spread across land and displace water.
- Destroying a source stops future production; it does not automatically erase Ooze already present.
- Ooze removal should follow the weapon or removal mechanic used. Water then flows into newly available space according to the hydraulic approximation.

### Source placement

- Place sources above the median **ground elevation** of the map, not by water-surface height.
- Five sources per level is the default target.
- Small or large maps may vary from three to seven sources.
- Placement should be reproducible from the map seed.
- Avoid impossible or unfair starts; leave a plausible opening strategy.

## 5. Terrain

- Represent the initial map as a grid.
- Every cell has permanent underlying ground elevation.
- Ooze and water never permanently alter ground elevation.
- Terrain should form useful channels, basins, ridges, and routes rather than random noise alone.
- Maps must contain at least 80% land; water occupies no more than 20%. Fully underwater maps are out of scope.

### Terrain Intensity

A future control adjusts elevation contrast, with a planned range near -100 to +100 and default 0.

- **0:** preserve baseline elevation differences.
- **Negative:** reduce elevation variation without automatically flattening the map.
- **Positive:** exaggerate elevation variation.

Conceptually: `gameHeight = referenceHeight + (baseHeight - referenceHeight) * scale`

At slider zero, `scale = 1`. The mapping from slider value to scale remains an implementation decision.

## 6. Water and Ooze

VEYAL uses a fast, understandable hydraulic approximation, not full scientific fluid dynamics.

### Layers and density

- Ooze is much denser than water.
- Water and Ooze occupy distinct vertical layers.
- Ooze accumulates beneath water in low areas.
- Ooze displaces water; it does not convert water into Ooze.
- If Ooze rises above the water surface, it can spread over land and push water elsewhere.
- Rivers may back up behind a blockage and find an alternative route.
- Water continues to flow unless physically backed up or constrained by the map.
- At coasts, Ooze may fill water areas and effectively expand land. This is a gameplay abstraction, not a claim of real-world accuracy.

### Minimum cell state

Each cell should independently track:
- permanent ground elevation;
- water depth or volume;
- Ooze depth or volume;
- terrain and structure metadata as needed.

Fluid surfaces are derived from ground elevation and the relevant fluid depth, according to an explicit layer model.

### Conservation and boundaries

- Conserve water volume inside the map except for explicit boundary inflows/outflows.
- Removing Ooze must not change ground elevation.
- As Ooze recedes, water flows into the newly available channel or basin; it must not remain suspended over empty space.
- Boundary flows must be explicit rather than accidental fluid loss.
- Ooze production is an explicit source-driven addition and is not subject to water conservation.

### Performance

- Prefer local redistribution and downstream updates where practical.
- Do not recalculate the entire map after every weapon hit or small cell change.
- Use fixed or bounded simulation steps and predictable update budgets.
- Add development diagnostics for fluid volume and performance.

## 7. Structures and energy

The first playable version needs a minimal economy and a small set of structures. Names and balance remain open.

Possible initial categories:
- energy production;
- energy storage or distribution;
- basic defensive weapon;
- source-targeting or area-clearing weapon;
- optional relay/support structure.

Placement, energy consumption, firing, range, and damage should be visible and understandable.

## 8. Map generation

- Generate maps from a seed.
- The same game version and seed should reproduce the same terrain and source layout, subject to documented algorithm changes.
- Generate meaningful landforms, basins, ridges, and optional rivers/coasts.
- Place sources after ground elevation is established.
- Validate land percentage, source count, and a plausible starting area.
- Later, allow players to copy, share, and save seeds.

## 9. Real-world terrain (later)

A future optional feature may let players search for a location and create a map from elevation data. It must not be required for ordinary play.

- Core game remains playable offline.
- Online lookup and elevation download are optional.
- Respect data-provider licences and usage limits; cache data locally when feasible.
- Display data source and limitations.
- Terrain Intensity adjusts imported relief.
- Convert imported terrain into the same grid format used by procedural maps.

## 10. Modes and community content

Potential later additions include campaign scenarios, survival mode, custom maps, map sharing, configurable difficulty, and community-created structures/rules. Prioritise the core loop first.

## 11. Initial win/loss conditions

For the first playable slice:
- **Win:** all active Ooze sources are destroyed.
- **Loss:** Ooze reaches a clearly defined critical area or overwhelms the essential base/objective.
- Both conditions must be visible in the interface.
- The precise defeat threshold should be tuned through playtesting.

## 12. Open design questions

Resolve through small prototypes and playtests:
- energy model and network connectivity;
- first structure set and costs;
- exact Ooze flow and removal equations;
- fluid simulation schedule;
- fair-start generation;
- defeat condition and difficulty curve;
- maximum practical map size.
